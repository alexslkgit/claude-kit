## Structure patterns

- Opener is almost always a direct address to the reader about how to use the piece: "If you are here for [X], I suggest scrolling down to [Y]. Otherwise I will explain...", this exact move appears near-verbatim in articles 1 and 2.
- Second paragraph usually states the motive for writing bluntly: "I built this because I wanted to know...", "I wrote this article simply because I couldn't find a similar one when I needed it."
- Uses named section headers as single words or short phrases: "Intro", "Analysis", "The Solution" / "The Bill" / "The Funnel", "Conclusion" / "Conclusions". Consistent across all three articles regardless of topic (technical how-to or narrative essay).
- Numbered lists used for taxonomies (e.g., three student groups, five AI tips) rather than for sequential steps.
- Images are used as evidence/illustration with the platform boilerplate "Press enter or click to view image in full size" caption, followed by a real caption line only in the data-heavy article (e.g., "The dashboard: what the validator let through. The bottom two rows are the promotions from 27 July."), the how-to and essay articles use plain stock photos credited "Photo by [Name] on Unsplash" with no further caption.
- No code blocks in any of the three pieces, even in the Xcode/Swift article, technical concepts are explained in prose with inline symbol names (@IBInspectable, @objc, layer.borderWidth).
- Heavy use of concrete numbers and dollar figures in the most recent (data journalism style) article; the two older articles are advice essays with almost no numeric data.

## Tone and voice

- First-person, direct, mildly self-deprecating about not knowing everything ("I am not yet a profound expert in it").
- States his own credentials explicitly and un-selfconsciously when relevant ("My name is Oleksandr, and I am a software engineer. I have a Master's degree...").
- Confessional asides are common: admits being wrong, admits a guess vs. a measured number, admits procrastinating five years before starting to write.
- Prefers plain declarative sentences over hedging; short one-line paragraphs used for emphasis and pacing, especially at section transitions.
- Occasional rhetorical question addressed to the reader ("Now tell me, how can this be compared to...?", "Will it ever be cheap?").
- Numbers are stated then immediately interpreted, not left to speak for themselves ("S6, Google Jobs, cost $4.27 over the period. That is 51.7% of the entire shard budget.").

## Sentence and paragraph length

- Sentences mostly short to medium (10-20 words); technical/data sentences are often clipped to a single clause for punch ("LinkedIn posts went nowhere.", "The connector died in silence.").
- Paragraphs are short, frequently 1-4 sentences; single-sentence paragraphs used deliberately for a beat or a turn in the argument.
- Longer, list-like paragraphs appear only when itemizing categories (student groups, AI tips, shard sources).

Article 2 (the how-to) omits the Discord pitch and keeps only the translation note, the Discord/community outro appears to be reserved for essay-style, non-technical posts.

## How to write like him: checklist

1. Open with a direct instruction to the reader on how to navigate the piece (skip to X, or read on for context).
2. State the personal motive for writing in the second or third paragraph, plainly.
3. Use short, single-word or three-word section headers (Intro, Analysis, Conclusion, or topic-specific like "The Bill").
4. Keep sentences short; break to a new paragraph often, including one-sentence paragraphs for emphasis.
5. When citing numbers, always follow with a one-line interpretation of what the number means.
6. Admit uncertainty explicitly when a figure is estimated rather than measured, rather than presenting it as fact.
7. Use numbered lists for categories/types, not for step-by-step instructions.
8. Avoid code blocks; explain technical symbols and terms inline in prose.
9. Caption images only when they carry unique data; otherwise a plain photo credit is enough.
10. End with a two-part outro: an invitation to his Ukrainian iOS Discord community (essays only) plus a note on the piece being translated from a Ukrainian original, with a link back to it.
